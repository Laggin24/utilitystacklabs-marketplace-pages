Processing details

Service, subject matter and purpose: on demand, use the signed-in user's Jira permissions to search visible subtasks outside the Done status category in one company-managed Jira Software project, read visible direct-parent version data, compare Fix Version ID sets, display the comparison and generate a CSV after a complete scan. The app is read-only and has no app report database or permanent report history.

Data subjects: users, employees/other personnel, Customer business contacts and other people identifiable in Customer-controlled version names, issue keys or associated metadata, as applicable. Customer Personal Data: selected project key and project metadata used for scope validation; issue/subtask IDs and keys; parent IDs and readable parent keys; Fix Version IDs/names; issue-type/subtask and status-category information; scan timestamps and comparison outcomes. Data categories include user-generated content, unique identifiers and associated metadata. The app does not request descriptions, comments, attachments, email or assignee fields for reports. Jira may include incidental metadata in response objects; the app returns reconstructed comparison fields.

Sensitive/special-category data: None/Not Applicable for the Service's intended scope. Such data is not sought or needed; Main Agreement Section 7.2 prohibits Customer submission of Sensitive Data. This does not guarantee that Customer-controlled version names or issue metadata can never contain sensitive information contrary to intended use.

Frequency: on demand when a user runs a report. Nature/purpose: permission-scoped reading, transient handling/display, version-ID comparison and Customer-directed CSV generation under Customer Instructions. Duration: an active report session and provision of Service under the Main Agreement, with end-of-processing treatment under DPA Section 8. Customer controls downloaded CSVs. Atlassian platform logs and Provider-controlled support/Marketplace records are described separately in the privacy policy.

Subprocessors, notices and security

Provider Subprocessor List and transfers: Atlassian Pty Ltd (Australia) for Forge hosting/compute and Jira API delivery of the report. Atlassian's current subprocessor list identifies downstream providers, functions and processing locations. Its corporate address does not mean all processing occurs in Australia. GitHub Pages hosts documentation, not customer reports. There is no external report-data backend or app report storage.

Subprocessor Notice Method: email to the Customer's Marketplace Order, billing or technical administrator contact, or a replacement data-protection contact designated to Provider. Provider will maintain its subscription to Atlassian's subprocessor-change notices. Customer notice delivery for this app has not been exercised.

Technical and organizational measures: Atlassian-hosted Forge and Jira authentication; signed-in-user API calls; only read:jira-work; no external report backend/storage; strict project-key, numeric-ID and cursor validation; internally constructed queries and fixed API destinations; response minimization including suppression of embedded parent metadata; bounded pages and parent batches; no CSV on incomplete scans; explicit unknown comparisons; text-only rendering; fixed issue-navigation paths; CSV formula neutralization; and no intentional logging of report rows, queries or credentials. Atlassian's published platform measures apply to its platform. No independent certification, provider-operated security infrastructure or deletion automation is claimed.

Additional Term

Solely for a new or replacement downstream subprocessor engaged by Atlassian Pty Ltd in providing Forge for the Service, and not engaged directly by Provider, Section 4.3's 30-day advance-notice minimum is replaced with a 7-calendar-day advance-notice minimum. Provider will maintain its subscription to Atlassian's subprocessor-change notices and notify affected Customers by the Subprocessor Notice Method as soon as reasonably practicable after receiving Atlassian's applicable notice and, in all cases, at least 7 calendar days before that downstream subprocessor begins Processing Customer Personal Data. For those Atlassian-controlled downstream changes only, the period in Section 4.4(a) for Customer to object in writing on reasonable data-protection grounds is 7 calendar days after Customer receives Provider's notice. Section 4.3's 30-day advance-notice period and Section 4.4(a)'s 30-day objection period remain unchanged for Subprocessors engaged directly by Provider. Section 4.4(b)'s resolution and termination remedy and the DPA's 48-hour Specified Notice Period for Security Incidents remain unchanged. This term does not limit mandatory EU Standard Contractual Clause, UK or Swiss rights or their precedence under the DPA.

The 7-day customer advance notice is a firm Provider commitment, not merely forwarding Atlassian's notice. No production-customer delivery has been tested for this app.
